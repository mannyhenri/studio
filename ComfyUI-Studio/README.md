# Studio

A studio app on top of this ComfyUI. Open **http://127.0.0.1:8188/studio** (or http://<this-PC>:8188/studio from another computer on your network).

- Every workflow saved as an app (`*.app.json`, made with App builder in the node graph) appears in Create automatically.
- Gallery reads `Pictures\Comfyui` (ComfyUI's output folder). "Move to trash" moves files into `_trash` there.
- Characters are stored in `user\default\studio_data\characters.json`; their faces and voices in ComfyUI's `input\studio` folder.

`mconv/` is the UI-to-API workflow converter from comfy-cli 1.23.0 (GPL-3.0), vendored unchanged except for import paths.

`web/vendor/three/` is three.js r187 (MIT, its `LICENSE` is there) with the add-ons Previz (`web/previz/`) uses, bundled into one file by `scripts/vendor-three.sh`; Studio serves it itself, so sets open with no internet.

## Previz

**Previz › Quick scene** makes a blank slate to direct from scratch: a stage (a studio cyc, an 8 × 6 m room or open ground, each with a floor in 1 m squares), a stand-in for the subject (or two people facing each other) and one camera; nothing moves until you add a move. **Previz › Add a .glb** takes a glTF Binary file: uploaded from any device (saved in `Documents\Studio Sets`) or a path on the PC (used where it is). Open it to direct the set in the browser (`web/previz/`: `viewer.js` the viewer and its panel, `shots.js` framing and moves, `lighting.js` lighting setups, `objects.js` stand-ins and the outliner):

- **Cameras** (the dropdown): the file's cameras (Blender: *Include › Cameras*; an orthographic one too), or, when a file has none, the camera rigs a Blender export leaves (empties named like a camera, aimed by their `TGT_` target, lens from the name). Each camera plays on its own: picking one goes to where its move starts, and the timeline runs (and loops) just while it moves, found where its keys or its rig's change (the dropdown says when: `moves 1:08–1:40`); **⟷ Whole timeline** plays the set's whole animation. A Studio camera plays its own move the same way. Play, scrub, ← → a frame, Space. **◆ Keys** shows a lane per camera under the timeline: each Studio camera's shot as a light clip with its move inside, from its start keyframe to its end one (◆ for keyframes), and the set's cameras and animation where they move. Drag the move's ◆ ends to decide when it starts and ends in the shot (a push in that starts at 3 s of a 10 s shot), drag the move to slide it, drag the shot to move it all in time, drag the shot's ends (just outside the clip) to change where it starts and ends; it snaps to 0.1 s, the playhead, the shot's ends and the other lanes (Shift: to the frame). Click a lane's name to look through that camera, click or drag the ruler to scrub. **＋** makes a Studio camera from the view. The frame (16:9, 2.39:1, 1.85:1, 4:3, 1:1, 9:16) keeps the lens's horizontal view, as Blender does with a 36 mm sensor.
- **＋ New camera** starts on the main subject: medium shot, front, eye level, 35 mm, with three-point light if the scene isn't lit yet; when someone stands in front of the subject (the other half of a dialogue) it goes round to ¾ or profile for a clean single. **✥ Operate** moves a Studio camera by hand, off the subject: drag to pan and tilt on the spot, right-drag to slide, wheel in and out, W A S D Q E to move it (Esc when done); it changes the move's start (or its end, once the move is over).
- **Subject / With**: the set's characters (found by their skinned meshes; their head and toe bones tell where they face), stand-ins you add, or any object you select. The **Subject** is who the shot is about: shot sizes, heights and sides are measured on it, lights are placed around it, POV looks through its eyes and a move that follows, follows it. **With** is a second person, used only by a Two-shot (both in frame, the camera across the line between them), Over the shoulder (past With's shoulder onto the subject; ⇄ Reverse swaps the two) and to pick the side of a group shot. Changing either moves nothing by itself: it counts the next time you frame (a Framing or Height button, or ⟳ Frame again now); moves and keyframes never use With.
- **Framing**: shot sizes (extreme wide, very wide, wide, full, medium full, cowboy, medium, medium close-up, close-up, big close-up, extreme close-up), kinds of shot (single, two-shot, group, over the shoulder, POV, insert), the side (front, ¾, profile, back, reverse) and the lens. The camera is placed so the subject fills the frame as the shot size says; closer than 0.5 m to a face it goes to a longer lens; a wall in the way keeps it inside and goes wider.
- **Height**: eye, shoulder, chest, hip, knee and ground level; high and low angle, overhead, worm's-eye, aerial; Dutch angle (roll); **Aim**: pan left or right and tilt up or down off the subject (◎ puts it back on the subject).
- **Moves**, by hand or a preset. **✥ Manual move**: the **Camera position** buttons move the camera itself as on a dolly or a crane, without turning it (↑ ↓ up and down, ← → left and right, In and Out along the floor; a step from 1 cm to 1 m, hold a button to keep moving); place it, ◆ Set start, move it, ◆ Set end, and it travels between the two (Editing ◆ Start / ◆ End picks which keyframe the buttons change; the panel says how far it travels). **⇢ Preset moves**: push in, pull out, dolly zoom (in/out), truck, pedestal, crane, pan, tilt, whip pan, arc, zoom (the position buttons move where it starts). Keyframes keep more than the position: ◆ Set start keeps everything as it is (where the camera is and looks, lens, roll, the tabs' settings, the light); then change anything (framing, height, pan and tilt, lens, light, or move the camera: operate it or fly the free camera; move the timeline to where the move should end, or type its Length) and ◆ Set end. Play animates all of it between the two, the light too (the same setup: its lights move and change; two setups: one fades into the other). With both set, a change goes into the keyframe nearest the timeline (Editing ◆ Start / ◆ End go to them). A camera with a move has a **shot**: the stretch of timeline it plays (◎) and a movie records, with a start and a length typed in seconds (any length, from 0.1 s to an hour; the Shot length slider covers the first 30 s). The move happens inside it, from its start keyframe to its end one (**Move ◆ … → ◆ …**, typed or dragged in ◆ Keys): the camera holds still before and after, so a 10 s shot can push in from 3 s to 10 s. Moving the keyframes never shortens the shot; a longer shot keeps the keyframes where they are (a move that filled the shot still does). A move has an ease, and can follow the subject or be handheld; **▶ Play the move** plays it with the set's animation, and **↺ Reset move** takes it away: the camera goes back to where the move starts (its position, framing, lens and light) and stays still.
- **Light**: the set's own lights (Blender's, brought back to its look), neutral, three-point, high key, low key, Rembrandt, loop, butterfly, split, broad, short, rim, silhouette, under-light, top light, golden hour, midday sun, overcast, blue hour, moonlight, candlelight, neon, emergency lights. Key, key:fill ratio, key side and height, colour temperature, ambient, exposure, shadows. Lights are placed around the subject from where the camera is.
- **Objects**: add stand-ins (person, child, box, pillar, wall, floor, steps, table, chair, car, tree) or a prop (any .glb, kept as a prop, not a set); click to select; double-click (in the view, the list or the top view) or **⌖ Frame it** brings it into frame in the free camera, its handles ready to move it, from where it can be seen (round it if a wall is in the way; closer and wider in a tight room); a double-click on the floor or a wall sets the pivot instead; move, turn, size (through the camera you look through too: adding one keeps you in that camera and puts it where the camera sees it); hide anything (H, or 👁 in the list: it stays hidden, and Show all brings everything back), delete what you added (Del), put a moved object from the file back where the file has it, or find it in the list.

- **◫ Top view**: the set from above, beside the view or under it (whichever leaves the view the bigger picture), as a floor plan: anything more than 2.4 m above the floor is cut away (ceilings, roofs), people are tinted, stand-ins keep their colours, every camera is drawn big enough to grab. Click to select, drag to move on the floor, Shift-drag to turn, drag a Studio camera to move it (you look through it as it moves; its move comes along), double-click an object to frame it in the view or a camera to look through it; wheel to zoom, right-drag to pan, ⌂ for the whole set. It and ◆ Keys stay open the next time you open the set.
- **📷 Snapshot**: a 1920 px picture of the view (no gizmos or guides). Make it the first frame of a storyboard shot (an existing shot, or a new one with a prompt written from the framing and the camera's move in the shot's camera words), or a **location shot**, kept with the set under the viewer (→ Storyboard shot later). Every snapshot is also in the Gallery (Images › Previz › the set).
- **🎬 Movie**: the camera and its move as an MP4 in the Gallery (Videos › Previz › the set), for reference: every frame is rendered at its own moment with the set's animation (720p or 1080p, 24/25/30 fps), so it plays at the right speed on any computer. The set's movies are listed under the viewer too.

Directing never changes the file: directing a set camera makes a Studio camera from it. Cameras, moves, lighting and objects are saved with the set (`direction` in `glb_sets.json`); the card picture comes from the viewer (🖼 uses the current view). Exported the .glb again over the same file? Studio reads it again by itself (or press ↻), and what you directed stays: objects and cameras are found by name.

Studio doesn't run Blender: a set made in Blender (or any 3D app) comes into Previz as a .glb.

## Models

Models (LoRAs, other downloads) and Settings › Models on this PC list only model files that are really there: an empty file (a placeholder, or a download that never started) or a download still in progress (`.part`, `.tmp`…) isn't listed.
